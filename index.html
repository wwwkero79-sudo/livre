Blood supply and hepatic circulation

The liver has two inflows. The portal vein (about 75%) brings nutrient-rich, lower-oxygen blood from the gut and spleen. The hepatic artery (about 25%) brings oxygen-rich blood. Percentages are approximate.

Portal vein + hepatic artery → sinusoids → central veins → hepatic veins → inferior vena cava → right atrium.

First-pass metabolismOral drugs are absorbed, travel through the portal vein to the liver, and may be partly metabolized before reaching the systemic circulation. This lowers oral bioavailability.

Bile formation and pathway

Hepatocytes make bile and secrete it into canaliculi. Duct cells add water and bicarbonate. Between meals, bile is stored and concentrated in the gallbladder.

Most bile salts are reabsorbed in the terminal ileum and return to the liver through the portal vein: the enterohepatic circulation.

Portal resistance and pressure

Effective liver function

Not a simple scarFibrosis changes how blood moves through the liver. That causes two problems: portal hypertension and hepatic dysfunction. Fibrosis may partly regress in some causes with effective treatment, but cirrhosis is not universally reversible.

Conclusion

The liver keeps metabolic balance, makes essential proteins, processes medicines and bilirubin, and forms bile. Chronic injury leads to fibrosis and nodules, which raise portal resistance and reduce function. For pharmacy students, that means medicine choice, dosing and safety must be reassessed in hepatic disease.
